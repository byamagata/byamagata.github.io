For years, I have been asked by cohorts of interns and associate engineers: "What is the best advice you can give to someone in my position?"

Often, they expect something technical—the one framework or design pattern that will make them a 10x engineer in months. But it's much simpler than that:

<p class="hero-text" style="font-size: 24px; font-weight: bold; text-align: center;">"Talk to Everyone"</p>

I learned this while walking the streets of Seoul, South Korea, as a missionary for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. My job every day was to approach strangers and overcome the gut-wrenching awkwardness of striking up a conversation about the deepest aspects of their lives.

In Seoul, that social courage was how I found the best food, the hidden hangouts, and the PC방s that didn't smell like smoke and had the best rates. But when I transitioned into tech, I realized this wasn't just a survival skill for a missionary—it was the key to unlocking everything in my career.

Talking to everyone is the key to unlocking your potential within your organization and the industry. Everything you want is usually hidden in plain sight, just waiting for a conversation to reveal it. When you stop being a hermit and start talking to people outside your immediate circle, huge things happen:

You make genuine friends across the entire industry, not just at your desk.

You get invited into "secret" or pilot projects before they are even officially open.

You finally understand what different business units actually do and why they matter to the bottom line.

You see how and why the big-picture company decisions are actually made.

You see people realize they actually prefer Product or Marketing over Engineering and make career pivots that change their lives.

Being a recluse, or limiting who you talk to because of social awkwardness, means you are choosing to stay blind to the opportunities right in front of you.

My challenge to you is this: Talk to someone new this week. Find someone who isn't part of your immediate circle or even in your specific organization. Ask them about themselves, what they do, and why they do it.

Do that, and watch doors start to open in front of your eyes.
